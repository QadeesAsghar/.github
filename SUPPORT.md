way to do that.

## Before asking

You will usually get a faster answer if you have:

1. **Searched the [docs](https://docs.slang.com)** — most questions are answered there
2. **Searched [existing discussions](https://github.com/slang/slang-community/discussions)** — someone has likely asked already
3. **Checked [open issues](https://github.com/search?q=org%3Aslang+is%3Aissue+is%3Aopen&type=issues)** — it may be a known bug

---

## Writing a good question

Include:

- **What you are trying to achieve** — the goal, not just the failing step
- **What you tried** and what happened instead
- **Versions** — SDK, widget, browser, Node
- **Error messages** — the full text, not a paraphrase
- **A minimal reproduction** if you can

A question with these gets answered in hours. Without them, it turns into a
round-trip that takes days.

| Channel | Typical |
|---|---|
| GitHub Discussions | 1–3 business days |

Slang is early-stage and maintained by a small team. We read everything, but we
cannot always reply immediately. Community answers are welcome and appreciated.

---

## Issues vs. Discussions

**Discussions** are for open-ended things: questions, ideas, feedback, showing
what you built.

**Issues** are for confirmed, actionable work with a clear definition of done.

Bug reports start as Discussions and are converted to Issues once reproduced.
This keeps the issue tracker a reliable picture of real work, rather than a
mixture of work and open questions.
